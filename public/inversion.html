New to Utah?

What is an inversion?

Normal day: air near the ground is warmest. Warm air rises and carries pollution away. It gets colder as you climb.

Inversion day: a warm layer sits on top of cold valley air, like a lid on a pot. Cold air can't rise, so car exhaust and smoke pile up underneath, day after day.

Salt Lake City sits in a bowl of mountains, so in winter (Dec–Feb, calm and clear days) the smog can stay for a week or more.

The local trick: drive up into the canyons and you pop out above the smog into blue sky.

Why it matters

Health

Fine particles (PM2.5) go deep into the lungs. Exercising in smog means breathing in much more of it — the opposite of why people go hiking.

Visitors don't know

Locals know to head up the canyons. Students, tourists and newcomers just see grey skies and either stay inside or hike in the smog.

The answer is nearby

Clean air is often a short drive up. The hard part is knowing how high to go — that's what this page estimates from live weather stations.
